# Geometry practice – November 24, 2026 (Quadrilaterals)

1. **Warm-up:** The interior angles of a convex polygon add up to 1980°. How many sides does the polygon have?
2. **Medium (algebra-in-figure):** Rhombus ABCD has diagonals AC = 24 and BD = 10 that intersect at E. Find the side length, the perimeter, and the area of the rhombus.
3. **Challenge (proof):** In quadrilateral ABCD, diagonals AC and BD intersect at E, and E is the midpoint of both AC and BD. Prove ABCD is a parallelogram. (Hint: find two congruent triangles first, then use alternate interior angles.)

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Sum = (n − 2)·180°, so (n − 2)·180 = 1980, n − 2 = 11, so **n = 13** sides.
2. A rhombus's diagonals are perpendicular bisectors of each other, so △AEB is a right triangle with legs AE = 12 and BE = 5. AB = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = **13**. Perimeter = 4·13 = **52**. Area = ½·d₁·d₂ = ½·24·10 = **120**.
3. (1) AE ≅ CE and BE ≅ DE because E is the midpoint of both diagonals. (2) ∠AEB ≅ ∠CED (vertical angles). (3) △AEB ≅ △CED by SAS. (4) AB ≅ CD and ∠BAE ≅ ∠DCE by CPCTC. (5) ∠BAC and ∠DCA are alternate interior angles for lines AB and CD with transversal AC; they are congruent, so AB ∥ CD. (6) One pair of opposite sides is both parallel and congruent, so **ABCD is a parallelogram**.

</details>
