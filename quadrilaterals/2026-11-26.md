# Geometry practice – November 26, 2026 (Quadrilaterals)

1. **Warm-up (algebra-in-figure):** In parallelogram ABCD, m∠A = (3x + 10)° and m∠B = (2x + 20)°. Find x, m∠A, m∠B, m∠C, and m∠D.
2. **Medium (coordinates):** A(0, 1), B(4, −1), C(6, 3), D(2, 5). Prove ABCD is a square, and find its area.
3. **Challenge (proof):** In quadrilateral ABCD (any shape), E, F, G, H are the midpoints of AB, BC, CD, DA. Prove EFGH is a parallelogram. (Hint: draw diagonal AC and use the midsegment theorem in two triangles.)

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Opposite sides are parallel, so ∠A and ∠B are consecutive interior angles (supplementary): (3x + 10) + (2x + 20) = 180, so 5x = 150 and **x = 30**. m∠A = **100°**, m∠B = **80°**. Opposite angles of a parallelogram are congruent, so m∠C = **100°** and m∠D = **80°**.
2. Slope AB = −2/4 = −1/2 and slope DC = (3 − 5)/(6 − 2) = −1/2, so AB ∥ DC. Slope AD = (5 − 1)/(2 − 0) = 2 and slope BC = (3 + 1)/(6 − 4) = 2, so AD ∥ BC. Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel: **parallelogram**. Slope AB · slope AD = (−1/2)(2) = −1, so AB ⊥ AD: a right angle, so it is a **rectangle**. AB = √(4² + 2²) = √20 and AD = √(2² + 4²) = √20, so adjacent sides are congruent: a rectangle with two congruent adjacent sides is a **square**. Area = (√20)² = **20**. (Check: both diagonals have length √40 and share midpoint (3, 2).)
3. (1) In △ABC, E and F are midpoints of AB and BC, so EF ∥ AC and EF = ½AC (Midsegment Theorem). (2) In △ADC, H and G are midpoints of AD and DC, so HG ∥ AC and HG = ½AC (Midsegment Theorem). (3) EF ∥ AC and HG ∥ AC, so EF ∥ HG (two lines parallel to a third are parallel to each other). (4) EF = ½AC = HG, so EF ≅ HG. (5) One pair of opposite sides is both parallel and congruent, so **EFGH is a parallelogram**.
